AstroScore daily

The next 16 days for every saved spot at once: a row per place, a column per day, and each cell two block glyphs, one per half-day. Same AstroScore (觀星 score) as astro-score_readable.html — this page just asks it of every place in the list at once (10 rows) and folds each day's 24 hours into two half-day blocks. The first row, 輸入, is the one that is not a saved spot: it follows the location box below, which 使用目前位置 fills from the device and 在地圖上點選 from a map. Read it to pick where and when, then open the readable grid for the hour-by-hour detail. Four forecast models come back in every request; the tabs pick which one the bars are scored from. No API key needed.

  • AstroScore (觀星) says the sky is dark, moonless and clear but knows nothing about where A* is; the core's altitude says the subject is up but knows nothing about the sky. Only both at once colour the strip, so a green bar with no purple under it is a fine night with the core down.
  • It is in degrees, so its ceiling is the core's own transit altitude — ~35.8° at the northernmost saved spot (大武崙砲台) to ~39.1° at the southernmost (龍磐公園) over Taiwan. The top two shades belong to lower latitudes.
  • Each block is half a day, so each holds exactly one dark stretch: the small hours fall in 00–11 and the evening in 12–23, while their daylight halves score 0 and cannot touch a maximum.
  • A night straddles midnight, so it reads across two cells — the right glyph of one day (that evening) and the left glyph of the next (its small hours). █ on the right of 08/18 next to █ on the left of 08/19 is one clear night. A single cell showing ██ is instead the tail of the night just gone beside the head of the one coming.
  • The best hour, not a total or an average — one clear hour is worth the drive even when the rest of the night clouds over, and a sum would average it away. It also means a bar says nothing about how long the window lasts: open the readable grid for that.
  • Hide past days (從今天開始) is off to start with, so the week past_days asked for is on screen from the first Fetch: a forecast beside what the sky actually did, with a heavier rule marking today. Tick it to drop those days. It drops whole days only, and never shortens a day it shows — today's column always covers the full 24 hours, hours already gone included, so a block does not shrink as the afternoon wears on. Hover any cell for the exact sums.
  • The score itself is (100 − Cloud) × (100 − MoonPenalty) / 100, zeroed while the sun is above −10°, MoonPenalty (月亮扣分) ramping 0 → 100 as the moon climbs 0° → 10°. Sun and moon are computed in the page. See astro-score_readable.html for the worked examples.
  • The models do not all reach 16 days out, and here that reads as a blank, not a dash: icon_global stops after about 7.5 days, jma_seamless after 11 and ecmwf_ifs025 after 15, while gfs_global fills all 16. Beyond its own horizon a model returns nothing and no hour is scored, which is not the same answer as a hopeless night — so those blocks draw a faint · rather than the blank a washed-out sky gets. Expect a field of them at the right end of the grid; hover for the (0/12 hr) count behind it.
  • Cloud (雲量) comes from one model at a time, picked with the cloud source (雲量來源) tabs above the grid. All four are fetched in the same request, so switching is instant and costs nothing — and disagreement between them is information: where jma and icon tell the same story the forecast is worth trusting, and where they do not, it is not. The default is icon_global; jma_seamless is the one to switch to for a spot in the Central Range, being the only one of the four with a ~5 km regional nest over Taiwan rather than an 11–25 km global grid.
