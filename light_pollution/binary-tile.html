binary tile

How dark is the sky at a lat,lon? This fetches one tile of David Lorenz's World Atlas of Artificial Night Sky Brightness and decodes it by hand, showing every step. It is the 光害 / Bortle / LP Zone rows of astro-score_readable on their own. No API key — the tiles are static files on GitHub Pages, which is the only reason a browser can read them at all.

tile = binary_tile_<x>_<y>.dat.gz   a 5° square, 600×600 points at 1/120° (~0.9 km) value = 2-byte corner + Σ 1-byte deltas   walk up the left edge, then across the row ratio = (5/195) × (e0.0195·value − 1)   artificial glow ÷ natural night sky SQM = 22 − 2.5·log₁₀(1 + ratio)   mag/arcsec² — bigger is darker
  • The file contains neither SQM nor Bortle — one logarithmically quantised integer per grid point and nothing else. Everything below the raw bytes is derived here.
  • SQM and LP Zone are the atlas's own; Bortle is not. Lorenz declines to publish a Bortle number, because Bortle judges the whole sky — light domes on the horizon included — which a zenith figure cannot see. It is shown because people speak it, never instead of the SQM.
  • The atlas is modelled from NOAA/VIIRS satellite night lights (processed by the Earth Observation Group, Colorado School of Mines), not measured from the ground.
  • Changing the year re-reads the same point from a different annual atlas, which is the cheapest way to see a place get brighter or darker over a decade.
